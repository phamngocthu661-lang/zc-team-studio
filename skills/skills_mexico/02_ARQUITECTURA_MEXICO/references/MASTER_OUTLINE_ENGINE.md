# MASTER OUTLINE ENGINE — Pacing, reveals, consequences

## Three-axis promise
1. OUTRAGE: viewer knows exactly what the family did wrong.
2. UNDERSTANDING: viewer gradually learns why the narrator tolerated it and what the family fears losing.
3. JUSTICE WITH CONSEQUENCES: narrator acts within believable rights and accepts an emotional price.

## First 5,000-body-character mandatory placement
- 0–200: live quote / rejection / event; example only: `—Ya le dejamos esa recámara a mi mamá —dijo Mariana...`.
- 200–450: objective proof such as suitcase, group-chat message, marked contract or boarding pass. Proof must be something lead can sense.
- 450–750: what can be lost right now (roof, marriage memory, family ties, savings).
- 750–1,300: protagonist asks precise question or refuses first coercive demand; micro-payoff #1.
- 1,300–2,300: antagonist responds, introducing a bigger obstacle; no background dump.
- 2,300–5,000: second verifiable clue + consequential immediate action; micro-payoff #2. Begin backstory only in pieces prompted by present action.

## Six evidence beats / four reinterpretation floors
E1 evidence visible early (impossible to ignore). E2 past sacrifice ledger. E3 first discoverable documentary inconsistency. E4 corroborating witness/secondary record. E5 antagonist counterevidence or honest grievance. E6 final document/action that makes the chosen reversal legitimate. Can include more if all functional.
R1 initial affront is real. R2 adult child is conflicted/complicit, not merely a puppet. R3 protagonist's boundary has a real cost. R4 the final truth changes who can trust whom without changing basic physical facts.

## Scene causality table (for each of 5–8 scenes in each Part)
`Scene ID | LOCATION/TIME | viewpoint | goal | who blocks | action/dialogue | new evidence | cost | state delta | closes loop | opens loop | words/chars estimate`. Use NEW action and NEW decisions, not repeated reflection paragraphs.

## Open-loop ledger
Track exactly what viewer expects to learn: L1 reason for betrayal; L2 proof of ownership or authority; L3 whether son knew; L4 protagonist's hard choice; L5 effect on grandchildren; L6 whether reconciliation costs something. Keep 3–6 active max. Close the title-level loop in Part6 and emotional loop by Part7; each closure must show evidence/action, not narrator exposition.

## Retention check by Part
- P1 unjust event and small first response.
- P2 concrete lost sacrifices and first contradiction.
- P3 tested first boundary and backlash.
- P4 midpoint reframing and uncomfortable sacrifice.
- P5 pressure strikes protagonist's deepest fear.
- P6 choice backed by proof, no magical rescue.
- P7 aftermath, moral distinction, behavior change; avoid four pages of generic moral lecture.

## Dialogue & internal voice
Show a scene then reflection, not reflection replacing scene. Lead's spoken Spanish has concise ordinary syntax. Avoid constant “me quedé helada”, “nunca imaginé”, “mi corazón se rompió” repeated. Use Mexican objects and sensory detail functionally: bolillo warming at morning table, mercado receipt, bus stop, folder of predial bills, telephone call during siesta; not all must appear. Respect varying Mexican life experiences; do not stage every family as wealthy.

## Counterargument matrix
For each decisive event, record (1) why person does it now, (2) what they know, (3) how it affects someone else, (4) better easier alternative and why it fails, (5) cost afterward. A drama ending can't rely on a character forgetting her own name, owning a random hidden bank or granting a one-hour eviction.

## V1.1 — Show reversal, not merely proof
An evidence chain makes protagonist's act **possible**, it is not itself the consequence. Plot must actively switch power/control, at a moral cost. For `Mi hijo vendió mis máquinas...`, ad discovery is E3, but P6's emotional peak must involve a grounded decision, buyer interaction, ownership claims, compensation/relationship choices, and a consequence; don't fake instant legal remedy. For `mis hijos se repartieron mi casa`, reading WhatsApp is evidence, but no one loses access or entitlement without a real decision within elder's authority.
The outline FAILS if its unique payoff is only `(a) she saw a message`, `(b) she asked a question`, `(c) everyone was shocked`. Every segment needs new interpersonal pressure and a concrete state change.
