# CHANNEL / TITLE DNA — OBSERVED PATTERNS

This reference summarizes patterns extracted from the user-supplied channel/title dataset. It is not a generic title guide; it is used only to classify the already-finalized title and to keep SEO/thumbnail/trailer aligned with audience expectations.

## 1. MODERN ABANDONED-PROPERTY / SECOND-CHANCE DNA

Observed in Silence's Narratives-style data.

High-performing patterns emphasize:

- a vulnerable adult woman or mother
- divorce, homelessness, loss, betrayal, or being left with little
- a **specific** property: lakeside resort, motel, boathouse, stagecoach inn, school, island house, workshop
- inheritance or very low-cost acquisition
- one concrete unexplained detail: extra key, cabin that should not exist, old shed, hidden boathouse clue, erased record

Packaging lesson:

**Specific property + human vulnerability + specific clue beats generic “house + changed everything.”**

SEO should support the exact property and story-specific clue.

---

## 2. FRONTIER / LOST-HISTORY MYSTERY DNA

Observed in Hearthstone Stories / Hidden Legacy-style data.

Common winning engines:

- lost convoy / wagon train / railroad car / mining camp / ghost town
- 80-100-year time gap
- first person to reach/find it
- specific abandoned or impossible object
- concealed cargo, sealed interior, erased map, hidden structure
- low-cost frontier acquisition as a secondary engine

Packaging lesson:

The unique historical object is the click engine.
Do not dilute it into generic “secret place.”

---

## 3. SURVIVAL / EXTREME-WEATHER DNA

Observed in Old Prairie Light-style data.

Winning engines:

- blizzard / below-zero danger
- cave, earth shelter, stone shelter, hidden floor, food cache
- protagonist mocked or cast out
- unconventional survival method later proves correct
- active physical problem solving

Packaging lesson:

The thumbnail/trailer should show **action + environmental threat + survival mechanism**, not merely a woman looking at a building.

---

## 4. GOTHIC ESTATE / INHERITANCE DNA

Observed in Forgotten Fables / Hidden Legacy-style data.

Winning engines:

- abandoned manor / estate / castle / cottage / orchard
- dead lord / duke / family / in-laws
- property judged worthless
- hidden legacy, sealed cellar, library wall, dry well, forgotten fortune
- emotional exile or unfair inheritance

Packaging lesson:

The estate must feel like a character, but the human emotional wound still matters.

---

## 5. RESOURCEFULNESS / DISCARDED-ASSET DNA

Observed in Plowline Legacy / Harvest Reckoning-style data.

Winning engines:

- unusually large quantity
- discarded or written-off material
- protagonist recognizes hidden utility/value
- practical transformation
- measurable payoff

Packaging lesson transferable to Homeless stories:

**Specific, concrete, visually legible objects outperform abstract promises.**

Do not import huge quantities or monetary payoffs into a Homeless story unless the script actually contains them.

---

# PERFORMANCE WEIGHTING PRINCIPLE

When channel data contains view counts, treat higher-view titles as stronger evidence.

Do not let many weak titles override a few clear high-performing outliers.

Use performance to identify proven mechanisms, not to copy wording.
