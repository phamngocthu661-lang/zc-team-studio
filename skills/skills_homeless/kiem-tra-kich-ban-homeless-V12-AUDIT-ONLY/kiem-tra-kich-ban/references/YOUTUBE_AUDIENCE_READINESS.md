# YOUTUBE + US 40+ AUDIENCE READINESS V1.5.0

## PURPOSE
Audit every script for platform suitability, hook strength, mature American audience fit, spelling/usage quality, and character/voice consistency. This is a publication-readiness layer, not a censorship engine.

## 1. SOURCE-OF-TRUTH RULE FOR YOUTUBE
YouTube policies and advertiser-friendly guidance change. If current official YouTube guidance is accessible, treat it as the source of truth.
Do **not** invent a static “banned words list.” Context matters.

Flag and repair material risk involving, at minimum:
- profanity / vulgar language, especially prominent/focal use and title/thumbnail/hook placement;
- graphic or shocking violence/injury/gore;
- harmful or dangerous acts presented as encouragement/instruction/glorification;
- hateful, derogatory, slur-based or demeaning framing;
- explicit sexual/adult content;
- self-harm/eating-disorder material;
- drugs/tobacco/firearms when focal or instructional in a way that creates policy/ads risk;
- exploitation of sensitive events/tragedy;
- deceptive/dishonest behavior framed as instruction or celebration;
- any content that could violate Community Guidelines even if monetization is not the only concern.

For dramatized/fictional storytelling, preserve necessary plot facts where allowed, but reduce needless graphicness, exploitative framing, gratuitous slurs/profanity, instructional detail, or shock-for-click wording.

### YouTube patch classes
- **YT-P0 — Community Guidelines risk:** must fix/remove/reframe before publication.
- **YT-P1 — likely advertiser/monetization risk:** repair unless user explicitly accepts limited monetization and policy still permits the content.
- **YT-P2 — avoidable sensitivity:** wording/context can be made safer without damaging story.
- **YT-OK — contextually appropriate fictional/historical reference:** preserve; do not over-sanitize.

## 2. HOOK — HIGHEST PRIORITY / EARLY RETENTION SURVIVAL GATE
The hook is not just the first dramatic line. Audit **click-to-story continuity**.

Hard opening checkpoints using script-body character positions:
- by ~200 chars: title-promise object/action/anomaly concretely present;
- by ~400: physical/verifiable proof;
- by ~650: why-it-matters-now stakes;
- by ~1,100: Micro-Payoff #1 answers/confirms something meaningful;
- by ~2,000: bigger question + protagonist next action;
- by ~5,000: Micro-Payoff #2 / first functional clue or reinterpretation.

Check additionally:
- no generic atmospheric preamble before title contact;
- no question-only hook with no early proof/progress;
- **NO HOOK RESET:** do not leave the clicked mystery for extended biography;
- before Micro-Payoff #1, standalone biography is forbidden;
- inside first ~2,000 chars, no continuous backstory/exposition >~500 chars without current action/evidence/stakes;
- no spoiler that collapses grand reveal;
- no repeated setup sentence;
- no vague AI lines like “what happened next changed everything”;
- clear forward action, not only narrator promises.

If any mandatory early function is materially late/missing, mark **HOOK REWRITE REQUIRED** or **REWRITE ENTIRE PART 1** while freezing strong lines/beats.

## 3. RETENTION — LONG-FORM YOUTUBE
For every Part ask:
- after ~5,000 chars, is there any ~6,500-char retention desert with no meaningful state change/payoff?
- what new question, leverage, threat, evidence, choice, or emotional shift occurs?
- where could a viewer leave without regret?
- are there 2+ consecutive explanatory/documentary beats without action or changing hypothesis?
- is pressure introduced early enough to create a clock?
- does each reveal open/reshape a larger question until the title-level reveal?
- are quiet scenes earning attachment/contrast rather than merely slowing pace?
- is the ending action-led rather than moral-explanation-led?

Flag dead zones even if prose is grammatically good.

## 4. DEFAULT VOICE — AMERICAN VIEWERS 40+
Unless user specifies another audience, target mature U.S. viewers 40+.

### Desired feel
- mature, confident, clear American English;
- “legendary / heritage” storytelling: history, place, labor, weather, land, family, craft, consequence;
- emotional dignity rather than adolescent intensity;
- concrete nouns and verbs; restrained figurative language;
- suspense through evidence and consequence, not constant hyperbole;
- moral weight emerges from choices/actions, not sermons;
- occasional dry humor is welcome when character-specific;
- respect for practical intelligence, work, responsibility, community, property/history, and earned competence.

### Avoid
- TikTok/Gen-Z slang unless character-specific and necessary;
- internet-speak, meme cadence, excessive “iconic/savage/insane/crazy”; 
- faux-Biblical/faux-archaic diction used to manufacture “legendary” tone;
- purple prose and melodrama every paragraph;
- therapy-speak or contemporary ideological vocabulary in historical voices unless period/context supports it;
- over-polished quotable dialogue from every character;
- excessive fragments and cinematic slogan lines;
- generic “life lesson” narration.

## 5. CHARACTER CONSISTENCY LOCK
For each recurring character freeze internally:
- age / role / background / education;
- physical condition and limitations;
- knowledge and evidence access;
- temperament;
- risk tolerance;
- money pressure/material incentives;
- values and loyalties;
- speech rhythm / vocabulary / humor;
- relationships and forms of address;
- what changes across the arc vs what should remain stable.

Flag any line/action that violates this lock without a causal turning point.
After rewrite, rerun the lock across the full script.

## 6. SPELLING / GRAMMAR / USAGE SWEEP
For English scripts aimed at the U.S.:
- standardize American English spelling unless a proper name/quoted source requires otherwise;
- correct typos, missing/extra words, duplicate words, malformed punctuation;
- check dialogue punctuation and quotation consistency;
- check capitalization of titles, places, institutions, historical documents;
- check subject-verb agreement, tense drift, pronoun antecedents;
- check homophones and near-word substitutions;
- check proper-name spelling every occurrence;
- check numbers/units/date formatting for consistency;
- do not “correct” intentional dialect unless it harms comprehension or becomes caricature.

Every real typo/grammar error is actionable even if tiny.

## 7. STRONG-MATERIAL PROTECTION
Before recommending large-scale rewrite, mark all material that already works:
- strong hook line;
- distinctive character line;
- clean clue setup/payoff;
- effective quiet human beat;
- nuanced antagonist exchange;
- strong reveal timing;
- vivid but grounded description;
- earned emotional action;
- ending image/callback.

Strong material must be placed in **MUST PRESERVE** and copied exactly during rewrite unless that exact span/dependency was explicitly included in the user-approved Patch Ledger.

## 8. PART REWRITE TEST
Mark **REWRITE ENTIRE PART** when one or more are true:
- >~35% of the Part would require interconnected edits;
- the Part’s causal/retention architecture is wrong, not merely local wording;
- character knowledge/motivation is wrong across multiple scenes;
- hook/title contract failure contaminates most of Part 1;
- fixing local patches would create obvious seams or redundancy;
- tone/audience mismatch is systemic throughout the Part.

A Part rewrite still preserves every locked strong span/beat.

## 9. FINAL PUBLICATION GATE
A revised script is not ready until:
- hook passes;
- no known P0–P3 actionable defect remains;
- no known YT-P0/YT-P1 risk remains unless user explicitly accepts a permitted monetization tradeoff;
- character voices/canon remain consistent;
- spelling/grammar sweep passes;
- US 40+ voice remains coherent;
- protected strengths remain intact;
- no new regression appears after two blind full reads.
