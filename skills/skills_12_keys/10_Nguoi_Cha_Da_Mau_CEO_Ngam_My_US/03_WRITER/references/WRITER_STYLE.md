# US SPOKEN NARRATION V2 — CONFLICT > PROCEDURE

Write clear conversational U.S. English for a 40–55-minute listening experience as dictated by actual word count/WPM. Scene-based prose, short meaningful dialogue, action details that can be heard; paragraphs generally 30–100 words, sentences naturally varied. Do not use production captions in TTS. Regional idiom must sound plausible, no ethnic caricature.

**Controlled mystery**: narrator/POV knowledge must not inadvertently spoil undercover identity. Two legitimate options: (a) mystery = audience learns with father; (b) dramatic irony = audience knows early and anticipation concerns how/when father learns; outline must declare which. Never accidentally mix.

**Retention differentiation**: opening cost; first 90-second clue; first verifiable contradiction by ~5 minutes; choice every 4–7 minutes; midpoint raises a new problem (not just more records), failure with emotional consequence; proof from 2 independent angles; realistic institutional remedy; father's family-life change, not generic billionaire reward. Use 7-part internal scaffold but hide transitions in seamless prose.

**Avoid**: abstract 'numbers versus people' repeated every scene, long back-to-back vendor accounting explanations, inexplicable CEO omnipotence, hero humiliated without agency, daughter drawn only for cuteness, rescue without lasting cost, exact same twists as channel references. Every antagonist must have a viable human motivation but a believable consequence. Show witnesses as people with goals, not merely evidence vending machines.

Proofread in en-US (American terms, consistent dates/pronouns), verify childcare/school-shift schedules, physical movement of objects, and what every character knows at each moment. For safe realism, avoid specific unsupported medical diagnoses, military techniques, legal timelines or aircraft repair steps.
