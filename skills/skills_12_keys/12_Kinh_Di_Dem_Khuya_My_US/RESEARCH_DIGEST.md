# RESEARCH DIGEST — USER-SUPPLIED US LATE-NIGHT HORROR EXAMPLES
Source: original 43-video user corpus, provided as reference research, retained from V1. No direct transcripts were supplied for all examples; this design does not claim line-by-line replication or independent YouTube metrics verification.

Strong format signal: atmospheric long-form anthology often with eight first-person stories and an everyday US setting (road trip, Airbnb, neighbor, camping, Halloween, night shift, small-town night drive, etc.). Core emotional mechanisms: ordinary objective, socially credible unease, an anomaly that can be checked, mounting constraint, choices that cost something, a concrete residue. Frame all new scripts as original fiction unless rights-cleared actual incidents are provided.

Avoid plagiarism and overfitting: do not reproduce competitor character names, turns of phrase, titles, plot sequence or thumbnail composition. Channel performance in sample is descriptive, not a promise of CTR, retention, monetization or published runtime. Use data to prioritize clarity and theme diversity, and production QA to test what is mechanically testable.
