# HUMAN PROSE & NARRATIVE SHARPNESS CHECKLIST V1.3

## A. DO NOT “HUMANIZE” WITH MISTAKES
Never add typos, bad grammar, false facts, or sloppy continuity to look human.
Human feel comes from specificity, asymmetry, motive, subtext, rhythm, restraint, and character-specific choices.

## B. LINE-BY-LINE AI-SIGNATURE AUDIT
Inspect every occurrence, not only global frequency.
An occurrence is actionable when a careful human editor would genuinely alter it because it weakens naturalness, clarity, rhythm, character voice, or retention.

Check:
- one-line narrative paragraphs used without real emphasis;
- unnecessary fragments;
- `Not X. Not Y. Z.` chains;
- `For the first time...` and similar formulaic openings;
- narrator slogans/moral lessons;
- repeated metaphor/motif;
- scene-ending aphorisms;
- characters sharing the same wit/cadence;
- dialogue questions followed by unnaturally complete explanatory answers;
- emotion shown, then explained again;
- artificial symmetry between unrelated/past-present characters;
- repeated abstractions such as “the building remembered / the room waited / the truth...”;
- em-dash used repeatedly as a drama engine;
- short triples such as `Water. Timber. Land.` when not earned;
- generic cinematic lines that could fit any story;
- exact thematic inscription/letter that exists mainly to state theme;
- perfect comeback lines in moments where a real person would hesitate;
- overly clean scene architecture: setup → clever line → reveal → aphorism every time;
- explanatory dialogue whose sole purpose is audience briefing;
- repeated transition patterns;
- repeated sentence openings or syntactic shapes;
- adjectives/adverbs that restate what the verb/action already shows;
- redundant emotional labels after physical behavior has already communicated the emotion;
- unnecessary “meaning” sentence after a strong concrete ending image.

## C. OCCURRENCE RULE
If a pattern appears 20 times:
- mark every occurrence that should be changed;
- explicitly preserve any occurrence that is effective and intentional;
- do not say only “reduce this globally.”

A single occurrence can still be P3 actionable if it is visibly artificial or weak.

## D. HUMANIZING MOVES
Prefer:
- actions with practical motive;
- incomplete answers;
- small hesitations;
- minor misunderstandings that resolve naturally;
- physical tasks during conversation;
- character-specific diction;
- silence/subtext;
- one concrete detail replacing several abstract sentences;
- mixed motives;
- small selfish/practical thoughts alongside noble ones;
- emotional payoff through object/routine/behavior;
- uneven conversational rhythm appropriate to each person;
- occasional ordinary phrasing between heightened dramatic beats.

## E. DIALOGUE — CHECK EACH EXCHANGE
Ask:
- Would this person really phrase it this perfectly right now?
- Does each character sound distinct?
- Is information being spoken only for audience convenience?
- Are jokes/repartee too frequent or evenly distributed?
- Does every conflict end with a polished thesis statement?
- Does the answer respond naturally to the actual question?
- Is subtext possible, or does everyone state intent directly?

## F. THEME
Weak/actionable:
- repeated moral summary;
- exact quote echoed across generations without causal reason;
- inscription exists only to state theme;
- narrator tells viewer the lesson after action already delivered it.

Strong/protect:
- related but different language;
- theme inferred from craft/choice/consequence;
- final action makes moral sentence unnecessary;
- motif changes meaning across the story instead of merely repeating.

## G. RETENTION SHARPNESS — MICRO + MACRO
Cut/compress/rewrite when a scene or paragraph:
- repeats information already established;
- supplies documents without changing hypothesis;
- explains a reveal after viewer already understands;
- adds atmosphere without pressure/clue/emotion/character value;
- delays title promise unnecessarily;
- repeats a reaction beat already paid off;
- uses three sentences where one concrete sentence carries the same force;
- weakens the next reveal by over-explaining the current one.

Do not make every sentence “high-retention.” Quiet contrast is part of human pacing.

## H. PURE PREFERENCE FILTER
Do not flag a line merely because another wording is possible.
Flag it only if the existing line has a real weakness a careful final-pass editor would change.


## I. MATURE U.S. 40+ VOICE CHECK
Unless audience differs, protect a mature American storytelling voice:
- grounded, concrete, dignified;
- heritage/craft/community/history atmosphere without fake archaism;
- suspense from evidence and consequence;
- less internet slang, meme cadence, trendy therapy jargon, or adolescent hyperbole;
- characters may use regional/occupational language, but voices must remain distinct and plausible.

## J. SPELLING / COPYEDIT PASS
Every typo, duplicated word, malformed punctuation mark, inconsistent proper name, grammar slip, tense drift, dialogue punctuation error, capitalization error, or unintended UK/US spelling mismatch is actionable.


# SATURATION REQUIREMENT V1.7.0
All actionable prose/TTS pattern occurrences must be enumerated during the first exhaustive audit. Do not save additional occurrences for later re-audits. Final saturation pass must find zero new actionable prose occurrences before score freeze.
