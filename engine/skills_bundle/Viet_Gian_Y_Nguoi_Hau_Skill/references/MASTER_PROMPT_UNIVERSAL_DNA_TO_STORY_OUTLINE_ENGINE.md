# PHASE 4 — RECEIVE NEW TITLE

When the user sends a new title, treat the title as a CONTRACT WITH THE VIEWER.

Do NOT immediately write an outline.

First decode the title.

Extract:

### 1. PROTAGONIST

Who is the emotional center?

### 2. SECONDARY POWER CHARACTER

Who holds money, authority, status, influence, protection, or social power?

### 3. INCITING EVENT

What specific event is explicitly promised?

### 4. CONFLICT

What is wrong or unfair?

### 5. POWER IMBALANCE

Who appears weak and who appears strong?

### 6. CURIOSITY GAP

What information is intentionally withheld?

### 7. IMPLIED TWIST

What reversal does the viewer expect?

### 8. EMOTIONAL PROMISE

What does the viewer expect to feel?

Examples:

- vindication
- outrage
- compassion
- romantic tension
- shock
- justice
- relief
- inspiration
- revenge
- healing

### 9. PAYOFF PROMISE

What MUST eventually happen for the viewer not to feel cheated?

### 10. TITLE ANCHORS

Identify details that cannot be casually changed.

Examples:

If title promises:

`Plus-Size Nurse`

the protagonist cannot suddenly become a waitress.

If title promises:

`at the wedding`

the inciting event cannot occur at a hospital.

If title promises:

`$2 Million`

the outline must meaningfully explain the $2 million.

If title promises:

`Widow's Daughter`

the family relationship must matter.

# PHASE 10 — HOOK ENGINE

The opening must be derived from:

**NEW TITLE + SELECTED DNA'S HOOK MATRIX**

not from a generic storytelling template.

Within the opening:

1. Deliver immediate evidence that the viewer clicked the correct video.
2. Introduce emotional tension.
3. Establish protagonist vulnerability or stakes.
4. Open at least one meaningful curiosity loop.
5. Avoid resolving the title too early.
6. Avoid long background exposition.
7. Avoid introducing too many names immediately.

The first hook should connect directly to the title promise.

# PHASE 12 — OPEN LOOPS

Design multiple curiosity loops.

## LOOP A — TITLE LOOP

Opened immediately.

Resolved near the climax/payoff.

## LOOP B — CHARACTER LOOP

A question about:

- past
- motive
- relationship
- hidden ability
- fear
- wound

## LOOP C — PLOT LOOP

A mystery, threat, object, financial clue, witness, document, secret, or unexplained behavior.

## LOOP D — MIDPOINT LOOP

Introduced around the middle to prevent retention collapse.

Do NOT open dozens of meaningless mysteries.

Every major loop must eventually close.

---

# PHASE 13 — ESCALATION RULE

Each major beat should increase at least ONE of:

- emotional stakes
- personal stakes
- financial stakes
- social stakes
- relationship stakes
- physical danger
- moral dilemma
- mystery
- urgency

Do NOT repeatedly recycle the same conflict.

Escalation should change the viewer's understanding of the story.

---

# PHASE 14 — MIDPOINT

The middle cannot merely be more events.

The midpoint should create one of the following:

- new evidence
- identity reveal
- betrayal
- unexpected relationship
- competence reveal
- hidden motive
- change in power balance
- unexpected consequence
- new threat

After the midpoint:

the story should no longer feel identical to the first half.

---

# PHASE 15 — CLIMAX DESIGN

The climax must resolve the story using elements established earlier.

Avoid deus ex machina.

The protagonist's:

- skill
- decision
- sacrifice
- evidence
- relationship
- courage
- knowledge

should materially contribute to the outcome whenever appropriate to the DNA.

The climax should deliver the emotion promised by the title.

---

# PHASE 16 — REALISM

When the DNA uses professional, historical, financial, medical, legal, technical, or occupational detail:

embed realistic details selectively.

Use them to create credibility.

Do NOT turn the story into a textbook.

The knowledge layer should serve:

**STORY + CHARACTER + RETENTION.**

Never fabricate false claims about real individuals.

If the story is fictional, keep it clearly fictional.

---

# PHASE 17 — CHARACTER ENGINE

Create characters who serve the title and DNA.

For the protagonist determine:

- age range when useful
- role/profession
- emotional wound
- immediate problem
- hidden strength
- competence
- moral boundary
- goal
- fear
- transformation

For the power character determine:

- public identity
- reputation
- private wound
- relationship to protagonist
- reason for noticing protagonist
- reason for respecting protagonist
- transformation

For the antagonist determine:

- goal
- motive
- leverage
- believable reason for conflict
- escalation strategy

Avoid cartoon villains unless the DNA specifically calls for them.
