# HORROR DNA A–F V1.1
A Boundary/Intruder: subtle breach of a supposedly safe space. Keep lock, path, access code and human visibility realistic.
B Social Dread: neighbor, acquaintance, stranger relationship has plausible leverage; never give villain impossible private knowledge.
C Isolation/Nature: weather, terrain, road condition and signal coverage create risk, not an arbitrary excuse to make the hero stupid.
D Transit/Road: lane geometry, mile markers, fuel, rest periods and sightlines justify decisions; US road infrastructure must plausibly match.
E Night Work: staffing, shift handoffs, access logs, equipment and procedures can both reassure and conceal; details must not become crime instructions.
F Ambiguous/Unexplained: establish bounds of unreliable perception; do not turn a grounded story into magic at the last paragraph.

Per story choose at least 2 unique physical/sensory motifs, 2–3 hard-evidence checkpoints and 1 meaningful protagonist choice. Diversify ending types (documented attempt, confirmed witness, harmless misread with threat elsewhere, narrow escape, escalation stopped, unproven anomaly, meaningful object return, morally costly departure). Do not recycle catchphrases or demand a dramatic killer for every episode. Seven-beat architecture is causality, not seven section headings in TTS script.
