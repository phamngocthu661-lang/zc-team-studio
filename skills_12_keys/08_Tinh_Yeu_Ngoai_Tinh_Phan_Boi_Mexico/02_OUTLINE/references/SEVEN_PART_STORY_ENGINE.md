# SEVEN-PART STORY ENGINE — RETENTION AND HUMAN PROSE

## Story scale: 7 part / 95–110 min
Suggested spoken-word planning budget (adjust so aggregate 12,500–14,500):
P1 1,700–2,000; P2 1,850–2,200; P3 1,900–2,200; P4 1,900–2,200; P5 1,900–2,200; P6 1,900–2,200; P7 1,500–1,900.
Assign a single concrete word target to each Part so total budget falls within range. Hard long-form gate is on the assembled script, not just planned budgets.

## Part-by-part beats / failures
P1: title event, active heroine, first burden of proof. Avoid revealing climax in Part1; do not postpone proof until Part3.
P2: social/career cost, authentic conversations, practical first protection; avoid villain monologue.
P3: new specific contradiction, partial answer, trusted person complexities. Avoid "she found another secret note" for no reason.
P4: adversarial countermeasure, emotional doubt with relevant agency, midpoint cognitive turn. Avoid improbable omniscient antagonist.
P5: heroine sacrifices something worth keeping; changes strategy after evidence and responsibility; avoid passive waiting for a man.
P6: confrontation in a properly seeded setting; witnesses/records credible; consequences proportionate, not instant legal victory.
P7: show differentiated emotional aftermath: changed daily routine, career, family or parenting; antagonist gets just consequence, not contrived severe punishment; romance optional and gradual.

## Scene clock
- Scene count typically 6–9/Part, 45–60 total. 1 scene has **conflict + action + decision + delta**. If all four absent, combine or remove.
- Estimated time per segment depends on Spanish voice; present in scenes as narrator beats, not speaker tags.
- Before each Part closing, pay one promise and advance one question. Every opened loop must have specific scene closure and evidence.

## Hook samples — generic and original illustrative fragments, not to copy verbatim
Example F1: "La invitación no llevaba mi nombre. Pero la firma al pie del contrato, la que él creyó que nadie revisaría, sí."  Opens concrete social humiliation and proof, not final solve.
Example F4: "No fue el mensaje de su amante lo que me hizo temblar. Fue ver la misma cantidad repetida en dos movimientos de nuestra cuenta." Evidence first, no instant omniscience.
Use emotionally specific verbs and clear causality; don't repeat these precise example sentences in generated script.

## Storytelling tone
First-person female narration is a strong default (`yo`) with occasional dialogue embedded in prose, but may choose close third-person if explicitly directed and hold POV consistently. Mexican Spanish, expressive but not overwrought. Limit cliché fillers ("nunca imaginé", "mi mundo se derrumbó") and repeated moral lectures. Alternate visceral detail with strategic action; vulnerability is allowed without flattening agency. Separate what narrator knows *then* from what she learned later.
