# OUTLINE ENGINE V1.1 — 7 PARTS / CAUSAL CHANGE / CHRONOLOGY

Produce ORIGINAL Mexican older-mother family drama, not a translation or clone of any competitor episode. Lock exact title first. A 70–80k character script succeeds on **nonrepeatable causes and character choices**, not on a string of moral lectures.

## Seven-Part architecture
| Part | Purpose | End state must change |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title promise delivered in first 90 sec; a concrete humiliation and narrator's choice | Home/access/relationship enters real jeopardy |
| 2 | Price of prior sacrifice; initial evidence tests the family story | Narrator obtains credible but incomplete information |
| 3 | Small practical counteraction; credible ally with limitations | Relationship, money or proof costs something |
| 4 | Midpoint reversal and opponent counteraction | An irrevocable consequence forces new strategy |
| 5 | Strong backlash and a **new** reframe, not re-confession; clarify what everyone already knew | Conflict shifts beyond the prior meeting preparations |
| 6 | Active and grounded resolution via documents/witness/decisions; protagonist also loses something | Visible legitimate power change without instant fantasy legal remedy |
| 7 | Compressed aftermath, realistic boundaries and emotional residue | Ending fulfills title without a second unrelated twist |

## Mandatory story-bible outputs in this exact order
`LOCKED_TITLE` and hash; `LOGLINE_ES_MX`; `FAMILY_GRAPH` with ages/alliances; `PLACE_AND_PERIOD`; `PROPERTY_RIGHTS_TABLE`; `MONEY_LEDGER` (amount, owner, actual access, date, source evidence); `TIMELINE_BY_EVENT_ID`; `KNOWLEDGE_MATRIX_BY_FACT_ID`; `EVIDENCE_CHAIN E1..E8`; `PRESSURE_AND_RETENTION`; `SCENE_CONTRACTS` 21–28 scenes (use stable `S01..S28`); 7 numbered `[PART N]` sections; `HANDOFF_FOR_WRITER`; `LENGTH_PLAN`; `OUTLINE_PREFLIGHT`.

## Required scene contract fields
For every scene, not just key moments, specify:
`scene_id`, `part`, `story_date` ISO (or relative date mapped to a specific ISO anchor in the bible), `time_of_day` morning/afternoon/evening/night, `narrative_order`, `story_chronological_order`, `flashback_signal` (if needed), `POV_access`, `active_opposition`, `protagonist_choice`, `new_event_or_evidence`, `state_before`, `state_after`, `causal_change`, `known_facts_before`, `known_facts_after`, `revealed_to_whom`, `cost`, `exit_hook`, `estimated_body_chars`.

If the narrator first hears a confession Wednesday evening in scene S14 but the Thursday phone call is narrated earlier in Part 4, label Thursday as **corroboration or refusal to admit publicly**, never a first confession to the narrator. Set clear flashback framing when Wednesday appears after Thursday in manuscript order. This is an inherited regression from MX_CUARTO Test 1 and a mandatory preflight.

## Reveal / repetition guard
Use stable fact IDs F1... and explicit FIRST_KNOWN for EACH key character. Before each alleged twist ask: Is this genuinely a new fact, new corroboration, new ownership status, or merely a rephrasing of what the listener already knows? Two consecutive scenes with no change of action, fact, control or relationship are not allowed. Never promise the family meeting at the end of Part4 and again at end Part5 with a third setup at start Part6 unless a concrete unforeseen event changed the meeting's stakes.

## Length allocation / pacing
Hard FULL 70,000–80,000 body codepoints; prefer 74,000–78,000. Design guidance **A 20,400–22,000**, **B 30,600–33,000**, **C 21,600–23,600**, but final gate is full length; if any stage deviates >10% from its plan, Writer must justify and compensate *with events*, not filler. Each scene must earn its word budget by doing one of: action, verifiable new information, change of allegiance, consequence, or transition required for clarity. Compress repetitious speeches and long post-resolution Part 7.

## Logic preflight questions
Who legitimately owns the home? Who only has keys/permission? What evidence is actually available on that day? Who saw or recorded private dialogue? How is money transferred with authority? What explicit action opposes the narrator? When and how does the narrator learn each fact? Does flashback framing keep the viewer oriented? Does the midpoint change stakes, not merely create another suspicion? Does final result obey Mexican regional law (flag unknown legal specifics for review)? Is the elderly protagonist fallible but genuinely agentic?

No scripted real-event claim without proof; no fabricated transcript, thumbnail or unverified audience metrics.
