## Audience promise (what the episodes deliver)
A mother or grandmother is treated as invisible or expendable, often in her own house or around a meaningful date. A hurtful action violates a boundary: being excluded from a birthday/wedding, being used as free childcare, pressure to surrender the house, unauthorized financial dependency, contempt for grief or age. The viewer follows her gradual gathering of facts, a costly choice, a visible reversal in control, and a bittersweet boundary/repair.

## Eight originalizable conflict families
1. `HOGAR_INVADIDO` — home use, keys, rooms and property permissions manipulated by family members.
2. `CUMPLEAÑOS_EXCLUIDO` — milestone slight / second family prioritized / a tangible expense or promise exposed.
3. `DINERO_SILENCIOSO` — repeated help, expenses and cards distorted into entitlement; audit receipts and access.
4. `CUIDADOS_OBLIGADOS` — grandma's care labor or recovery after surgery taken for granted; physical stakes.
5. `BODA_Y_MESA` — public humiliation at a wedding, anniversary or family gathering; witnesses and consequence.
6. `VIUDEZ_Y_HERENCIA` — grief exploited for access or documents; local legal/legal-aid realism.
7. `SECRETO_DE_FAMILIA` — old bill, document, recording or mistaken assumption recontextualizes family history.
8. `DIGNIDAD_SIN_VENGANZA` — seemingly small boundary set with large but lawful impact, empathy without weak ending.

## High-retention emotional design
`cold-open offense / quote -> specific wound -> narrator's quiet decision -> concrete test -> inconvenient proof -> deeper cost -> antagonist counteraction -> reversal at price -> moral ambiguity -> earned boundary + residue`.
- Every 5–8 minutes of narration should advance an observable state: new evidence, meaningful action, money/access change, confrontation consequence or changed personal allegiance. *Do not create fake mini-cliffhangers merely by withholding facts.*
- First five minutes: familiar Mexican domestic setting; precise antagonist action; narrator's immediate constraint; one evidence object; a choice that makes the viewer ask *¿qué hará ahora?*.
- Antagonists require a comprehensible desire, social leverage and limits. The son is not a robot who agrees with the daughter-in-law every time; show shame, mixed motives and independent choices.
- The narrator is not infallible. She may be proud, withholding, frightened, conflicted or partially complicit. Her dignity comes from choices, not endless wealth.
- Build 2–3 causally earned reversals, not 7 disconnected shock revelations.
- Emotional payoff: rights/keys/finances, a new living situation, a genuine cost, an honest conversation or credible distance; avoid false instant family reconciliation.

## Localization / voice
First person past tense es-MX, conversational but literate: clear varied sentence rhythm suitable for 70–110 minute TTS; select one believable region/city and maintain consistent details (colonia, mercado, camión, recibo, caja de ahorro, comprobante). Dialogue uses Mexican Spanish naturally without caricature, forced interjections or gratuitous CAPITALIZATION. Names and relations consistent (mi hijo, mi nuera, mi consuegra, mis nietos). Stay specific; do not claim full legal certainty.

## Explicitly forbidden cheap tricks
No copied source story with synonym changes. No unbelievable instant sale/bank freeze/court verdict, secret millionaire as default; no perfect revenge fantasy detached from property law, no omniscient grandma, no random poison-for-shock, no harmful stereotypes, no lectures every scene, no retelling the same insult five times, no implausible 1,000-word monologue, no unsupported “Historia real” assertion.
