# Competitor-inspired three-thumbnail concepts — image-safe version

## Observed visual DNA (reference images, not allegations)
- 720×404 reference images (~16:9), dark background, portrait(s) dominating upper 60%, large saturated yellow text spanning lower area, small white subtitle, yellow circular inset or arrow as attention bridge. Red/burgundy accents, black text stroke, high face contrast.
- Common pattern can be highly clicky but cluttered. Improve readability: 1 hero face, at most 1 secondary inset and 1 single clear arrow, title text 3–5 words and subtitle 4–9 words, no third dense line.
- Thumbnail A = persona versus dated public career moment; B = documented controversy versus quote/report explicitly attributed, not fabricated screenshot; C = emotion/legacy involving factual known performance or object.

## Full prompt construction (for image generator)
```
Create an original 16:9 editorial documentary YouTube thumbnail for a Spanish-speaking Mexican audience. Dark burgundy-to-black cinematic background. Right 38-42% dominant respectful portrait of [named celebrity, depict only if reference/rights allow]. Left 25% yellow-ringed inset containing [authentic documented public event/photo with rights OR neutral illustrative symbol], with single curved yellow arrow. Lower band big bold condensed yellow uppercase text with black outline: "[EXACT 3-5 SPANISH WORD HEADLINE]". Bottom smaller crisp white subheadline: "[EXACT 4-9 WORD SUBLINE]". Readable on phone. Genuine documentary aesthetic, avoid evidence-style false screenshots, avoid invented real-life arrests, medical diagnostics, tears or intimate scenes. If an archival depiction cannot be verified, use neutral illustrative imagery and do not label it archival. No extra text, logo, watermark.
```
- Actual photo rights/copyright clearance are separate production obligations; user-supplied sample thumbnails cannot be republished as originals automatically.
- Prompts may describe a historical figure but must not claim staged visuals are authentic documentation. For living figures, don't create defamatory visual composites indicating crimes, drugs or cheating with no evidence.
