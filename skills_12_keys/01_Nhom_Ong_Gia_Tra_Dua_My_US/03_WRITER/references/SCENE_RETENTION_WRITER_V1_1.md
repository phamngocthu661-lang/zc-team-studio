# STAGE A/B/C WRITER — SCENE DRIVEN V1.1

## Before writing each STAGE (only while drafting, never Step5)
Read locked title, exact outline, state-change grid, earlier persistent segments, identified cliffhanger receipts and open loops. Make sure the next scene changes `WANTS / KNOWS / RISKS / CAN_DO`. Do not keep adding identical remorse and legal explanations just to reach character minimum. If prose cannot fulfill the outline, STOP and flag inconsistency instead of inventing a fact that changes locked canon. Do not rewrite a previously SHA-persisted segment behind the manifest.

## Hook and promise mechanics
First 80–150 English words: physical setting + specific family insult/betrayal + the father's restrained but costly response. Tease one sharply defined question. Do NOT announce the entire final proof or summarize Parts 4–6. If initial scene establishes that the old man owns a building, that's acceptable; retain later surprises in the **conditions and consequences** he can prove and choose, not by unrealistically hiding ownership itself. When replaying the opening incident, clearly transition back in time or continue with NEW actions, don't quote the same speech twice without a narrative reason.

## Human pressure, not legal lectures
Write encounters, decisions and receipts. Legal/procedural accuracy should be embedded in: a precise request, rejected offer, signed form, cost estimate, withheld permission, genuine deadline, reaction from the person affected. A lawyer should clarify stakes once; she should not repeat the same ownership distinction across three conversations. A scene about a contract must show what the father decides next or what another character can now do. Under no circumstances portray facts as binding Ohio law without specific verified support; contractual terms are story facts, not general legal advice.

## One-scene editorial self-check BEFORE saving the current new segment
1. What specific action happened on-page rather than in summary?
2. What changed for at least one person? Could the scene be replaced with one sentence and no information lost?
3. What would the listener ask at this point, and is the answer earned within the correct Part?
4. Did the scene repeat a legal, money or grief fact without adding evidence/decision/consequence?
5. Does the narrator only report firsthand observations or information identified by its later source?
6. Does the scene honor title-promise timing and the emotional stakes rather than artificially delaying basic facts?
7. Is location consistent with the user's locked city and mundane logistics?
8. Does a child/grandchild have independent needs and not serve as leverage alone?
If substantive failure: repair the current *unstaged draft* before the stage is saved. This creative shaping is part of Step3 drafting, NOT the Step5 authorized repair transaction.

## Scene variation and chapter handoff
At least one non-document action scene per Part (real work, family conversation, public interaction, obstacle). At least one concrete consequence scene in P5/P6 and one earned reconciliatory or boundary action in P7. Avoid 3 consecutive scenes of bookkeeping/letter/review. Each Part exit leads to a specific costly next choice, not seven identical 'I didn't know what would happen' endings.

## Part-level soft budgets
Read the outline's recommended 15/14/13/15/15/16/12% split; honor it when story events permit, but 70k–80k overall and 7 headings remain the *only* deterministic size gates. For manuscript around 75k, target P1 11.25k, P2 10.5k, P3 9.75k, P4 11.25k, P5 11.25k, P6 12k, P7 9k. Don't pad text or remove essential emotion just to hit proportions.

## Stage boundaries
- A: fulfill opening wound, seed real mechanism and opponent motive; preserve unanswered major lever, don't dump final agreement details.
- B: make a first action fail, deliver midpoint plan change, then worst-day cost; avoid mere lawyer visits and documents without physical outcomes.
- C: execute actual credible consequence and show affected person's reaction; aftershock changes how family interacts; final prop image/line pays off P1.

## Read-only diagnostics
Use `python scripts/narrative_diagnostics.py FULL_SCRIPT.txt --expected-location 'Locked City' --out RETENTION_DIAGNOSTICS.json` at Stage C and Step4 as evidence to aid editing. This cannot detect semantic pacing, fraud, originality, CTR, retention or reader satisfaction. `FLAGS` are *editorial review prompts*, NOT automatic QA defects and must never be passed into Step5 as discretionary polish.
