# US Father Revenge — Script Craft Rules

**Quality priorities:** (1) hook/title payoff (2) earned emotional stakes (3) coherent money/ownership/authority (4) each chapter changes what father knows/can do (5) compression of repeated regret monologues (6) human voice and varied emotional beats.

**Narrator:** age ~55–75. Vary father jobs: retired machinist, school custodian, union electrician, small business founder, insurance adjuster, nurse, farm owner, municipal records clerk, aircraft mechanic, interpreter, auditor, former investigator. The job gives limited *realistic* competence (not blanket legal access). Use American vocabulary and idioms; avoid overusing 'my blood turned to ice', 'little did they know', 'they went pale', 'the room went silent', 'I smiled knowingly'. Do not claim all content is true: fictional stories may be inspired by universal feelings; transparency in description is required.

**Cold open:** reveal physical scene and precise wound in first 80–150 words; let a micro-action/line carry the drama. Narrator says what he saw, heard, or later verified. No 600-word generic autobiography before hook.

**Causal fabric:** One clue sets up next. A surprise must have both setup and reason it was unseen. Remember time zones, travel, funds, signatures, corporate structure, will/trust rules, witness competence, tech screen permissions, name spelling and ages. Avoid instant bank seizure/illegal forced eviction; actions father can legally choose are most powerful.

**Story heart:** The family antagonist may have a motive—status anxiety, greed, shame, coercion—but not all need redemption. At least one innocent bystander shows consequence; one real personal sacrifice from the narrator. Emotional release is not merely a flex of wealth.

**TTS:** prose has no Markdown bullet lists within Parts, no stage directions, no brackets beyond `[PART N]`; limit long nested clauses; natural contractions; stable American English spelling, money $, dates, geographic references; dialogue clear with who's speaking. CTA at most one tasteful paragraph after story conclusion.

**Important:** Plot, dialogue, names, objects must be new. Do not replicate Italian villa/fork sequence, economy ticket scanned ID, CEO scrubbing toilets, retired NCIS exact identity, bride wedding + staff taken, son six-year silence, or other copied sequence.
