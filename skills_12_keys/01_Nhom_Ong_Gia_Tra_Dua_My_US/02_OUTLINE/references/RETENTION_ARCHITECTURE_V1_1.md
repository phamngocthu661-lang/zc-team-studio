# SEVEN-PART SCENE / RETENTION ARCHITECTURE — V1.1 (STEP2)

**Do not substitute short teaser labels for a complete outline.** Add the fields below to the same current outline file; keep its existing lock header, exactly seven `[PART N]` labels, original ID and `LENGTH_PROFILE`. New fields are extra plain-text planning data; old scripts need no migration.

## Stage 0 — Write what makes the title fulfillable
`TITLE_EVENT`: promised insult/betrayal in scene (not merely referenced).
`PROMISE_PAYOFF_EVENT`: exact Part and scene where the elder's right/skill/proof changes another character's options visibly.
`OUTCOME_PROOF`: physical, relational or financial event showing the outcome is real and not just a lawyer's hypothetical.
`MORAL_COST`: what the narrator loses, risks, or concedes.
`NOT_A_VILLAIN_CARICATURE`: one credible motive/constraint for opponent, and one boundary the father cannot cross.

## Stage 1 — Seven-Part outcome map
| Part | Dramatic job | Minimum state transition |
| 1 | Start with humiliating incident and immediate counter-question | the father **chooses** a response that has a cost; hold back the full consequence, not the basic event |
| 2 | Build years of care + real motive; seed mechanism and warning | new artifact/witness changes what narrator suspects |
| 3 | First attempt to protect himself or another fails | antagonist makes an irreversible move; father loses easy route |
| 4 | MIDPOINT: new proof forces a changed plan | father's understanding/goal changes, not a second explanation of the same paper |
| 5 | Worst day: the personal cost arrives | a consequential bond, income or access is lost; cost felt in a scene |
| 6 | Justice: father executes his **lawful** decision | antagonist/bystanders encounter on-page/on-mic effect of precise promise payoff |
| 7 | Aftershock and deserved emotion | new behavior verifies changed boundary/relationship; recurring prop resolves |

## Stage 2 — Every meaningful scene needs a change
For each of 3–5 purposeful scene units per Part (21–35 scenes overall), record:
`SCENE_ID`, day/time/location, `WHO_WANTS_WHAT`, obstacle, physical action, new information, who can prove it, `STATE_BEFORE → STATE_AFTER`, concrete mini-payoff, unanswered question, promise link, authority/POV check. A scene with no change in agency, evidence, risk, or relationship is a candidate to merge or cut BEFORE writing.

## Stage 3 — Pressure / information cadence
Do not force random sensational twists every X seconds. For LONG_V12, design a meaningful reveal/decision/consequence roughly every 500–900 spoken words; for COMPETITOR_CUT, often every 350–700 words. Use as an editorial alarm, never an absolute validator. Alternate: humiliation → attempt → cost → document/witness → decision → reaction. Each legal/technical explanation must affect an immediate scene objective; never run 3 consecutive exposition scenes without a new decision or setback.

## Stage 4 — Calibrated body character proportions (SOFT, not hard byte gates)
Suggested weights P1 15%, P2 14%, P3 13%, P4 15%, P5 15%, P6 16%, P7 12%; allow approx ±3% individually when earned by scene load. These are NOT mandatory and must not conflict with exact total codepoint gate. Avoid scripts in which first two Parts are around 38% of length and last two under 20% unless there is a strong causal reason.

## Stage 5 — Explanation and surprise budgets
- One full explanation of any lease/trust/loan/authority mechanism. Later mentions should deliver new proof, condition, decision or real consequence. Record `EXPLAINED_ONCE_IN` and `EACH_LATER_USE_ADDS` in outline.
- Plant every decisive power/evidence tool by Parts 1–3 or mark why discovery in Part4 is credible. Do not invent a magic deed, secret will, or omnipotent attorney in Part6.
- Keep some empathy for son/daughter without negating their accountability.
- Reversal ladder: public misjudgment → father obtains credible actionable proof → proof applied with **human-visible consequence**. Do not reveal all three steps at one time.

## Stage 6 — Local specificity and voice
Lock a state/city and two to four *usable* local anchors (street geography, seasonal weather, neighborhood transaction, time to travel, locally typical labor/business detail). Place them in scenes organically; do not sprinkle placenames unrelated to action. Verify niche facts if needed. Local anchors do not justify invention of current law or local official detail.

## Step2 PRE-WRITE GATES (all must be justified before saving outline)
`PROMISE_GATE`, `STATE_CHANGE_GATE`, `RETENTION_GATE`, `CLUE_PROVENANCE_GATE`, `LEGAL_REALISM_GATE`, `LOCAL_ANCHOR_GATE`, `MORAL_COST_GATE`, `END_PROP_GATE`. Mark `PASS` with scene IDs/evidence or `REWORK_OUTLINE`; never fabricate PASS, and do not advance to Stage A with a preflight failure. Gate decisions are narrative editorial, not Python-certified audience metrics.
