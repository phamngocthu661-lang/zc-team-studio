# MASTER SCRIPT ENGINE — MEXICAN EMOTIONAL LONG-FORM

## Listener contract
The listener should feel (1) a concrete unfairness, (2) the everyday weight behind it, (3) satisfaction from an action that has a real cost. A woman isn't respected because she turns out to be secretly wealthy. She is respected because she stops surrendering choices and proves a fair claim.

## Voice guide
- Write in believable first-person literary oral Spanish, Mexican idiom but regionally understandable. Narrator has age and education and a consistent personal cadence. A retired teacher might say "mis alumnos"; a market vendor knows about invoice books and morning suppliers. Don't write every grandmother with the same clipped wise-quote voice.
- Scene action before moral reflection: `Mi nuera dejó una maleta al pie de mi cama. Sobre la colcha puso una etiqueta con mi nombre. No supe si reírme o preguntar cuánto tiempo llevaba planeándolo.` Original example only.
- Specific sensory details reveal memory and stakes: kitchen crockery from marriage, the small notch in a tabletop, receipt folded under a magnet; reuse symbolic object with new meaning by Part7.
- Dialogue contains interruptions, withholding, self-contradictions, concrete questions and plausible apologies. Avoid five-page monologues, courtroom-like speeches, uniform perfect sentences.
- Narrator may be angry, proud, stubborn, lonely, afraid to lose a grandchild. Balance dignity and vulnerability; no perfect revenge avatar.

## Beat-to-scene expansion
Use Outline's scene order and causality; for each scene write:
1. Spatial/time orientation, what protagonist physically does.
2. One immediate want and resistance.
3. A two-sided dialogue or action with local specificity.
4. One observation carrying evidence without convenient expository dump.
5. Decision, loss, or new obligation.
6. A meaningful final line raising next action or revaluation.
Natural atmosphere serves character and a later payoff, not word count. One repeated conflict cannot expand 15,000 chars.

## Part-specific expectations
P1: no delayed hook; establish age context via action, not autobiography; protagonist makes first boundary, clue in hand.
P2: show family history, concrete financial/ownership arrangement, sympathy for mixed motives; protagonist faces possibility loss is permanent.
P3: practical investigation/consultation, early proof, first no that changes family dynamics; adversary actively adapts.
P4: midpoint: evidence undercuts narrator's assumption or exposes child's participation; protagonist pays personal price and changes tactic.
P5: pressure escalates in realistic sphere (withdrawal of invitation, manipulative message, disputed document, social pressure), not cartoon threats; narrator chooses truth over easy approval.
P6: climactic confrontation/meeting/notary/bank/household choice. Every outcome has a grounded channel of authority. Reveal answers title conflict but doesn't magically erase emotional harm.
P7: months/days afterward grounded chronologically, the first changed routine, some people accept boundary, some don't. Narrator has chosen what family now means; last image mirrors beginning with altered meaning.

## Midpart rhythm safety
Every scene should advance at least two of these: plot facts, emotional state, agency, material stakes, relationship alignment, audience hypothesis. If a paragraph repeats two known facts without new feeling or action, cut. Avoid generic "el sol entraba por la ventana" more than story function allows.

## CTA policy
There is NO need for subscribe intro in story. If a creator-specific CTA is required, put one after the first real payoff, short and in narrator's voice, and one concise end question; never interrupt evidence chain or add template-y comments about a hypothetical viewer doing dishes.

## Scoring before artifact save
First 5k hook/micro-payoffs; 7-Part scene execution; all ledgers stable; financial/legal realism; Mexican voice read-aloud; antagonist and elder agency; no transcript artifacts; earned ending. Writer may self-check but cannot replace independent QA.

## V1.1 dramatic comparison gate
Every long script must have **both** a concrete unfair act comparable in legibility to the source benchmark and an *original* earned consequence for the offender. The template `insult → she discovers paper → everyone shocked` is not sufficient. Demand the physical scene in which the narrator refuses, renegotiates, legally protects, confronts or changes her own plan, and show what she loses. Add antagonist pushback with plausible interests and a flawed but sympathetic elder. Do not clone competitor courthouse/judge surprise; use lived Mexican causality.
