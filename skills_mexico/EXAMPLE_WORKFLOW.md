# EXAMPLE — one original fictional Mexican family drama (NOT a real audience result)

## LOCKED_TITLE (illustration only)
`Mi nuera dio mi recámara a su madre; esa noche busqué las escrituras`

## Story identity
Lead: Teresa, retired 67-year-old public-school teacher in Puebla. Son: Javier, 39, married to Laura, 36. Consuegra: Patricia, 64, in temporary housing after sale of her former apartment. Teresa and her late husband own the family house in a legal ownership state requiring confirmation by a professional; Javier and Laura occupy a guest room by permission, not under an assumed lease without evidence. Teresa has disability concerns and isolation; Javier feels obligated to help Patricia, but avoids the conflict and tells half-truths. All relationship labels and geography stay stable.

## Causal seven-Part plan (illustration, not the full STEP2 deliverable)
- P1: Laura measures Teresa's bedroom as if it has been gifted to her mother; Teresa discovers a moving van deposit receipt. Teresa says no but Javier insists that the plan is settled.
- P2: A history of Teresa's family sacrifices and home caregiving emerges through photographs and repayment folders, not passive autobiography. She asks Javier precisely what promises he gave Patricia.
- P3: Teresa reopens her old home papers, discovers a missing updated folder, asks notary's office which originals are on file rather than declaring instant victory; Laura threatens to withhold casual family visits.
- P4: Midpoint reveals Javier tried to solve Patricia's actual housing insecurity without telling Teresa and has paid a nonrefundable deposit. Teresa admits they once failed to ask what Patricia truly needed.
- P5: Patricia becomes an independent character: doesn't want to be a burden and did not know Teresa hadn't agreed. Laura still claims the house is a family resource she can allocate. Teresa refuses pressure despite emotional cost.
- P6: Teresa convenes an honest family conversation with consulted paperwork and practical housing alternatives. A credible written occupancy understanding is established (not magical eviction). Javier accepts financial responsibility for his promises.
- P7: Teresa wakes in her own bedroom, makes breakfast, and hears the first unpressured visit from her son; reconciliation is conditional and slow. Patricia finds an independently chosen arrangement. The final symbolic object is Teresa's simple house key set back on its hook.

This example shows the right story DNA (quiet dignity, material proof, adult responsibility), not a plot to copy word for word. ## V1.2 EXAMPLE — corrected abandoned mother / signature theme
**Proposed test-only TITLE (unlocked):** `Mis hijos me dejaron en la terminal de Puebla… 7 días después me suplicaron una firma`.
- Elder mother: a named 72-year-old Mexican woman, established first-person narrator, holds particular legal authority for a pre-existing, well-defined matter (exact document and authority must be verified/constructed by Step2).
- Her **adult children** deliberately choose to abandon her at the bus terminal; they remain culpable even if spouses have influence. Do not change crime or emotional conflict to unknown strangers.
- Their request seven days later has a plausible trigger and a specific paper they truly need (not mysteriously valuable land, sudden billionaire status or arbitrary magical notary intervention).
- Her P6 action is deciding whether to sign, **based on her own rights and values**; P7 shows actual costs of both refusal and any conditional consent.
- Forcing a judge to declare hidden inheritance before the story earns it is an invalid hook payoff. On missing Mexican legal verification, writer describes consultations/uncertainty, not fabricated statutory certainty.
