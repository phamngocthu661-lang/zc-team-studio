# HOOK → PAYOFF 7-PART ARCHITECTURE — COMPETITOR-LEVEL EMOTIONAL INTENSITY, NOT COPY

## Conversion from STEP1 TITLE_HOOK_BRIEF
The writer must not substitute a smart discovery for the **specific consequential action** sold by the title. The Step2 outline begins with a one-to-one `PROMISE_OWNERSHIP_MAP`:
`title clause | where audience sees it (P/scene) | observable offense | what narrator risks | earned leverage | decisive action | counterpressure | resolution scene | emotional price`. Each material phrase receives an actual scene, not merely reported retrospective narration. The 'but/then' clause should change relational power, not only provide a receipt.

## MEASURED DRAMA, NOT SHOUTING
- **Outrage:** an unmistakable human choice injures the elder. If the offender is the son, show his complicity; do not scapegoat only nuera/consuegra.
- **Humiliation with asymmetry:** a family member claims space, status or money the protagonist has earned. Viewer can picture physical gesture and exact person preferred instead.
- **Dignified agency:** the elder makes a nonreversible or costly decision in a sphere she really controls. Agency appears in P1–P3, not only P6.
- **Antagonist competence:** opposing character counters logically with an argument that could tempt narrator, not the same insult repeated louder.
- **Earned power reversal:** show a BEFORE and AFTER balance of control: access, voluntary payment, family narrative, property decisions or relationship boundaries. Only named lawful power changes hands, and paperwork/procedures take plausible time.
- **Emotional justice:** ending changes routines, not merely courtroom shouting; losses don't vanish once a family member apologizes.

## 7-Part escalating confrontation map (must appear in every OUTLINE)
| Part | Irreplaceable fresh beat | Observable reversal / price | Unanswered question driving next |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | The title betrayal plays ON STAGE, no delayed setup | Narrator demands a concrete answer, makes one small action | What did offender really choose? |
| P2 | Unequal treatment proven by DIFFERENT evidence, not more of same | Past sacrifice becomes disputed resource; narrator gives up an easy illusion | Is this a pattern, and who benefits? |
| P3 | First real boundary enacted, antagonist adapts | A relationship or cash/access cost arrives; cannot simply reset | Who has enforceable authority? |
| P4 | Midpoint personal betrayal or self-knowledge reframes her plan | Her initial hypothesis fails; she chooses more costly truth | Will she still act after this loss? |
| P5 | Closest bond or strongest fear is leveraged against her | She declines tempting false peace; consequence is irreversible | What proof or choice can change the balance? |
| P6 | Promised reversal/collision actually occurs with earned leverage | Rights/relationships meaningfully shift within lawful process | What will choosing dignity cost after victory? |
| P7 | Real aftermath without magical full reconciliation | A new ordinary action proves boundary held; some relationship uncertainty remains human | Close material loops, allow ordinary grief |

## REQUIRED SCENE-LEVEL PRESSURE TESTS
For every scene specify: desire, counterdesire, concrete action, irreversible delta, evidence provenance, cost, new question and exactly WHAT would be missing if scene deleted. If a scene can be cut without altering relationship, practical stakes, an inference or a later decision, CUT or FUSE it. At least 2 major credible counter-moves from antagonist, 2 changes of narrator's strategy, 1 seemingly comforting choice declined for a moral reason, 1 plausible nonvillain objection. These are quality checks, not quotas that override a locked premise.

## EMOTIONAL CONTRACT CHECK
Write a `VIEWER_EMOTION_WAVE` per Part: incident → controlled anger → doubt → cost → new self-respect → grief. Do not make every Part 95% anger. Include moments of warmth, individual memory, embarrassment, contradictory love and concrete work. This gives 70–80k codepoints an evolving inner story instead of a single extended argument.

## CLOSURE AUDIT
Ask what the headline's `...pero` promised. If final payoff can be summarized as 'she had the papers all along', enhance it with a costly CHOICE and a HUMAN consequence planted before. Avoid final-scene magical property transfer, instant jail, universal humiliation, private revenge with children, or a secret wealthy sponsor. Climax must show protagonist steering events.
